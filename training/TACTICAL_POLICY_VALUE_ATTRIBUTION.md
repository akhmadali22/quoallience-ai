# Tactical Policy-Value Failure Attribution

## Objective

Determine whether the low defensive policy priors and poor defensive child
values observed in the two-position per-action counterfactual audit are
systematic or specific to those two nearly mirrored positions.

This is an inference-only experiment. It does not train or modify the checkpoint,
does not run MCTS/PUCT, and does not edit the production search implementation.

## Position sampling

The sampler runs deterministic, seeded legal random games from the initial
Quoridor state. It uses the engine's legal pawn and wall actions. Pawn choices
favor shortest-path progress 72% of the time; when walls remain, the sampler
chooses a wall action 32% of the time. No neural policy or heuristic AI is used
to select sample positions.

A sampled root must satisfy both conditions:

1. The opponent (the player who moves after the root action) has at least one
   legal pawn move that wins immediately.
2. At least one legal root action removes every such immediate winning reply.

The sampler deduplicates complete states and selects positions round-robin
across buckets defined by wall count, root player's shortest-path distance,
and opponent pawn flank. It retains at most three positions from one playout.
These are engine-legal synthetic playout positions, not a claim about the
frequency of such states in competitive games.

## Evaluation

For each selected position, the script:

- evaluates the raw neural policy logits, masks to legal root actions, and
  applies softmax;
- enumerates every legal root action;
- labels an action defensive only if the opponent has an immediate winning
  pawn move before the action and has none after it;
- evaluates all non-terminal child states in batches with the same checkpoint;
- converts child values to the root player's perspective by negating the
  opponent-to-move value; terminal children use exact +/-1;
- ranks defensive actions against all legal actions for both policy prior and
  child value.

No search budget, PUCT score, MCTS visit count, category prior, root noise, or
search heuristic is involved. This isolates the two network heads.

## Primary metrics

- Percentage of positions with a defensive action in policy top 1 / top 5.
- Percentage of positions with a defensive action in value top 1 / top 5.
- Defensive policy mass per position.
- Best defensive action's policy and value rank.
- Best defensive child value minus the best non-defensive child value.
- Mean defensive vs non-defensive prior and child value.
- The same aggregate metrics by diversity bucket.

The best defensive action is used for rank metrics because a position can have
multiple distinct valid defenses. All legal action rows are retained in the
JSON and CSV for independent inspection.

## Run

From the repository root:

```bash
python -m training.evaluate_tactical_policy_value_attribution \
  models/checkpoints/exp_001r_vonly_strict.pt \
  --positions 80 \
  --seed 20261119 \
  --max-games 2500 \
  --max-plies 160 \
  --batch-size 128 \
  --device cpu \
  --threads 1 \
  --output-dir data/diagnostics/tactical_policy_value_attribution/run_001
```

For a smaller smoke run, use `--positions 5 --max-games 250` and a fresh
output directory. The full run refuses to overwrite a non-empty output folder.

## Outputs

- `tactical_policy_value_attribution.json`: complete states, all action-level
  prior/value scores and ranks, sampler settings, checkpoint hash, and pooled
  aggregate.
- `tactical_policy_value_attribution.csv`: one row per legal action.
- `summary.json`: pooled aggregate and per-diversity-bucket metrics.

The checkpoint SHA-256 is recorded in the report. Compare it with the prior
experiment before interpreting the results.

## Interpretation cautions

- The sample distribution is generated by a fixed random-playout policy. It
  provides diverse legal tactical positions but is not an estimate of how often
  these threats occur in self-play or tournament games.
- The immediate-win test is exact for the next opponent pawn move. It does not
  solve the full game or identify all strategically best defenses.
- A low defensive prior is direct evidence of policy misranking. A low child
  value is direct evidence of value-head misranking on the one-ply counterfactual
  children. Neither result alone establishes how a deeper search should behave.
- Do not change the checkpoint, loss, architecture, or PUCT based only on this
  diagnostic. First inspect pooled metrics, diversity buckets, and representative
  action rows.
